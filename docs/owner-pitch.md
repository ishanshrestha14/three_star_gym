# Pitching the website to the gym owner

Written 2026-10-07. The owner may think a website is pricey or useless, or may never have thought about
one. Don't sell "a website": sell **more members, and less time spent answering the same questions**.

## 1. Lead with cost

Get the objection out of the way in the first minute.

- **Running cost is close to zero.** Hosting (Vercel) and the database (Supabase) are on free plans, and a
  `.com.np` domain is free with the business registration.
- **The work is already done.** It's live at <https://threestargym.vercel.app>, not a quote for something
  that might happen.
- What it needs is someone to keep it updated: real photos, prices and posts. If you charge for that, say
  so plainly and keep it small and monthly; that builds more trust than a big upfront number.

## 2. Show, don't explain

Do it on **his phone**, because that's how his customers will see it.

1. **Google search:** standing near the gym, search "gym near me" and "gym in Manamaiju". Show who appears
   on the map and who doesn't. If competitors show up with photos and reviews and he doesn't, that one
   screen does most of the pitch.
2. **His gym's site:** open the site. Tap WhatsApp (it opens a chat with a message already filled in), tap
   Call, show the free-trial form.
3. **The admin panel:** show him changing a price or a photo himself in 30 seconds. Owners worry about
   depending on a developer for every change.

## 3. Talk in his numbers

Skip "SEO", "traffic" and "conversions".

- "If this brings in **one extra member a month**, it has paid for itself many times over." Use his real
  monthly fee.
- "People decide which gym to visit on their phone, before they walk in. Right now they can't see your
  equipment, coaches or prices, so they pick the gym they *can* see."
- "Every 'what are your prices, what time do you open' call or message gets answered by the site, day and
  night."
- "Enquiries land in one list with name, phone and what they want, so nobody gets forgotten."

## 4. Ask for something small

Don't ask him to "commit to a website". Ask for:

- **15 minutes to set up his Google Business Profile.** It helps him even if he never cares about the site
  (see `docs/local-seo.md`).
- His **real phone and WhatsApp numbers, hours and prices**, and permission to take **20 photos** of the
  gym and coaches on a quiet morning.
- A **3-month trial**: if he's not getting enquiries through it, you take it down. It costs him nothing.

## 5. Objections

| He says | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "My members come from word of mouth." | "Yes, and when a friend says 'try Three Star', the first thing they do is Google it. This makes sure they find you, with photos and good reviews." |
| "Websites are expensive." | "This one's built and hosting is free. The only ask is real photos and correct prices." |
| "Nobody looks at websites, everyone's on Facebook." | "Keep Facebook. The site links to it. But Google Maps and Google search show websites and reviews, not Facebook posts." |
| "I don't have time to manage it." | "You don't have to. I'll update it. If you want to change a price yourself, it takes 30 seconds from your phone." |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | "Try it for 3 months. We'll count the enquiries in the admin. If there are none, we take it down." |

## Before the meeting

- [ ] Put a few real gym photos on the site, even from your own phone. Seeing *his* gym on screen beats
      stock photos.
- [ ] Check how the 2–3 nearest gyms look on Google Maps.
- [ ] Bring a mock-up of a front-desk review QR code. It makes the idea concrete.

## After the meeting

- [ ] Real name, address, phone, WhatsApp, hours and prices entered in Admin → Settings and the CMS
- [ ] Google Business Profile started (`docs/local-seo.md`, section 1)
- [ ] Trial start date and how enquiries will be counted, written in the decisions log below
